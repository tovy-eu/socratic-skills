# Socratic reply

Goal: a reply that keeps the colleague thinking, instead of a reply that shows what the user thinks. One message in, one question out.

This is step 1 of a set of three. `socratic-question-check` audits a question the user drafted themselves; `socratic-conversation` prepares a whole conversation. Method background is in [references/socratic-method.md](references/socratic-method.md); read section 8 (pitfalls) and 9 (open, closed, why) when formulating, section 3 (attitude) if the user seems to want to vent or win.

## Input
What the colleague said or wrote, plus optional context and what the user wants from the exchange. Reply in the language of the colleague's message. Short, direct sentences. No filler, no praise.

## Steps

1. **Ask or say?** If the user actually wants to say something (an opinion, a boundary, a complaint), write it as a plain statement and offer the question as an alternative. A statement with a question mark fools nobody and poisons the conversation.

2. **Listen from the you-position.** Read the message asking "what do you mean exactly?", not "what do I think?". Note the type of utterance (fact, claim, judgment, defence), the central concept in one word, the small words that leak an agenda (*but, just, always, all, again, not yet*), and any contradiction or anecdote used as proof. Separate what the colleague observed from what they interpreted.

3. **Hold the reflexes.** Advice, help, "I had that too", convincing, nuancing, reassuring: each one stops the colleague's thinking. Register emotion without confirming or denying it. If the colleague plainly needs empathy first (grief, stress, something personal), offer one short empathic line before the question and say so.

4. **Pick the direction.** Down (facts: who, what, when, what was said literally) when the film is not clear yet. Up (what does X have to do with Y, what makes this bad) once it is. When unsure, go down; the colleague will drift up by themselves. Aim for the hot spot: the exact moment the irritation or judgment started.

5. **Build one question.** In order of preference: echo their exact words ("What was 'a whole hassle again'?", or just "All?"); "Tell me, what happened?" for a short or grumbling colleague; a naive request for explanation ("How does that work exactly?"); a concept question ("Where does ownership end and micromanagement begin?"); a suppose-that question with their own words, only once their view is understood.
   Formulation: one question, short, self-contained ("What do you mean by 'not scalable'?"). Open questions start with who/what/where/which/when/how; a verb-first question is fine only for a fact check. "Why" is good in a safe relationship; otherwise "What is the reason that...", "What made you...", "How did you arrive at...". No *but*, no two questions, no option pair where more options exist.

6. **Permission when sharp or personal.** "I'm curious how you see this. May I ask a question about it?" / "A question comes to mind and I'm not sure I should ask. I'll put it down; you decide." The colleague may decline.

## Output

```
**What I hear** – 2–4 lines: utterance type, central concept, leaking words, observation vs interpretation.
**Reply** – ready to send. One question. Optional one-line opener. Warm, informal, in the colleague's language.
**Alternatives** – at most two: one down, one up (or one echo, one suppose-that).
**Don't** – one line: the reflex the user probably felt, and why to hold it.
```

Keep the whole answer short; the user reads it between tasks.

## Example

Input (colleague on Teams, Dutch): *"Die refinement was weer zinloos. Niemand had zich voorbereid en de PO praat alleen maar over deadlines."*

Output:

**What I hear** – Two judgments ("zinloos", "niemand voorbereid") and one observation ("PO praat over deadlines"). Concept: voorbereiding, or maybe eigenaarschap. "Weer" leaks: this is a pattern for them. Whether nobody prepared is interpretation; what was said is fact.

**Reply** – "Zinloos is een stevig woord. Op welk moment in die refinement dacht je: dit heeft geen zin?"

**Alternatives** – Down: "Wat zei de PO precies over de deadlines?" Up: "Wat maakt voorbereiding voor jou het verschil tussen een zinvolle en een zinloze refinement?"

**Don't** – The reflex is to agree ("ja, dat herken ik") or to fix ("zullen we een voorbereidingstemplate maken?"). Both end the thinking; the question keeps it with the colleague.

## Reminders
- A question is an invitation. It stays with their story and sets thinking in motion.
- Irritation after a good question is normal. Don't soften the next message into advice.
- "That's obvious" or "you're making it complicated" means you are close. Ask calmly: "Why is it obvious?"
- Not disagreeing is not agreeing. The user's own view comes later, over a bridge (see `socratic-conversation`).

Never log unasked. If you can write files here, end your answer with one line: "Say 'log it' to save this to your log."
